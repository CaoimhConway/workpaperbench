# Reddit liquidation proceeds reference

The hypothetical transactions use issued capital at December 31, 2023 and the
pre-IPO rights. They are not a reconstruction of the IPO. Proceeds mean cash
available for the listed stockholders after all excluded claims. The scenario
explicitly equates that amount with the valuation used for the mandatory
conversion trigger and assumes the required transaction approvals.

## Cash distribution derivation

Let `n_i` be a series' original issued shares in millions, `q_i` its converted
common shares in millions, and `L_i` its contractual cash preference in USD
millions. From s01 and s02,

`L_i = n_i * issue_price_i * preference_multiple_i`.

All conversion ratios are one in the original dossier, so `q_i = n_i`. This
equality does not turn voting power into cash ownership. Original Class A and
Class B common holders own 7.099700 and 53.904204 million shares, respectively.

At consideration `V <= 240`, A remains preferred. A-1 is junior to the other
preferred series. The senior preference sum is 1847.3107574753 and A-1 has
0.6799733214, for a total of 1847.9907307967. Distressed senior distributions are
proportional to full claims. Neither original common nor the junior A-1 receives
cash in the original offers at or below 240.

At `V > 240`, s03 automatically converts A and A-1 regardless of whether doing
so improves their payout. The remaining preference sum becomes
1812.3103199753. Common shares before any other series converts become
`61.003904 + 7.500000 + 0.114746 = 68.618650` million. Until the remaining
preferences can be funded, common receives zero. This gives A 4.547207320700245
at V=240 and zero at V=241. A more valuable transaction can reduce a cohort's
cash entitlement under the actual contractual trigger.

Once remaining preferences are fully funded, let `p` be residual dollars per
common share and `C` the common count after mandatory conversion. For a
remaining preferred series, retaining its preference gives `L_i`. Conversion
gives `q_i * p`, and s03 prevents double participation. Conservation therefore
requires

`V = C*p + sum(max(L_i, q_i*p))`.

The right side is continuous and strictly increasing in `p >= 0` because C is
positive. Thus the common payout is unique. This equation is a reference
derivation, not a supplied candidate recipe. It does not apply to allocating
an underfunded preference pool, where contractual priority governs instead.

The executable reference starts with all remaining preferences funded and
releases each preference when its conversion payoff is larger, in ascending
`L_i/q_i` order. The reference SQL considers ordered prefixes. The accepted
alternative SQL examines all subsets, including non-prefix subsets. The minimum
candidate common price yields the unique conserved allocation. No inclusion
witness is graded, so ties between equal terms are harmless.

## Minimum funding and feasibility

Analyze the closed branch `0 <= V <= 240` and the open branch `V > 240`
separately. Within each branch, owner cash is nondecreasing. For an unconverted
preference holder with target T no larger than its preference, the first funding
point is senior claims plus `T * same_rank_total / own_preference`. Above its
preference, the holder must receive common proceeds. For original common or a
forced-converted cohort, use `p = T / owned_common_shares` and the conserved
funding amount. Intersect each result with its branch and choose the smallest
feasible boundary. Preserve whether that boundary belongs to the feasible set.

| Target | Proceeds infimum, USD million | Attained |

| A receives 10 million | 1903.801853308633 | 1 |
| F receives 54 million | 240.000000000000 | 0 |

| Original common B receives 2000 million | 5328.082614605810 | 1 |

For the first target, `4 * 1847.3107574753 / 35.0004375` is below 240 and pays
exactly 4. No smaller consideration funds that target in either branch. For the
second target, A's largest first-branch recovery is only 4.547207320700245.
After mandatory conversion, p must reach `10/7.5`, requiring
`1812.3103199753 + 68.61865*(10/7.5)`.

F receives 53.266616485340144 at 240. Its right-hand limit immediately above 240
is 54.295333731267995 because A's preference disappears. All consideration at
or below 240 fails the 54 target, while every sufficiently small positive
increment above 240 succeeds. Thus 240 is the sharp lower boundary, but it is
not a minimum. No tighter lower boundary is supported. The remaining targets
are met exactly at their stated values and fail immediately below them.

## Claims, alternatives and controls

Claim 1 is contradicted by A's drop between the 240 and 241 offers. Claim 2 is
supported by A-1's zero cash at 240 and positive converted payout at 2500.
Claim 3's assertion of zero actual award cash settlement is unresolved because
the retained award aggregates lack every award-specific settlement term.
Excluding awards in an authored scenario does not establish that actual
settlement is zero.

The focused test independently enumerates complete conversion patterns using
exact rational arithmetic. For each pattern it pays actual remaining claims by
rank and distributes the residual. It then tests whether changing any optional
cohort's conversion decision would improve that cohort's cash. Every surviving
pattern agrees with the reference. This checks the financial choice and priority
logic separately from the common-price equation and SQL implementation.

Nine changed-input checks cover row order, proportional scaling, book-value
invariance, lower and higher mandatory triggers, a changed junior rank, a larger
E preference, a changed E conversion ratio and a shifted F target. The raised trigger exercises optional
conversions while A and A-1 are still entitled to remain preferred. The target
shift changes the F boundary from open to attained. The 1.2 E conversion ratio
changes common ownership while retaining original preferred share counts and
cash preferences. Cash per original E share differs from cash per converted
common share. Both SQL paths and the independent exact waterfall check cover
this distinction. Synthetic inputs are controls,
not new issuer observations. Claim verdicts remain scoped to the original dossier.

Targeted mistakes include using a weak inequality at the mandatory trigger,
ignoring mandatory conversion, treating everything as already converted,
substituting carrying value for preference, putting A-1 in the senior rank,
paying both preference and residual, holding the common denominator fixed,
marking every funding infimum as attained and ignoring conversion ratios. The
ignored-ratio submission passes original inputs and fails the ratio control.
Numerical tables and cited claims are
verified. Optional explanatory prose is not semantically graded.
