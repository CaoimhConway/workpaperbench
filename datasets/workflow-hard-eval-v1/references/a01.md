## Certificate model

Let U be receivables plus regular-inventory advance value, P the unrestricted
perishable-inventory advance value, S the unrestricted script advance value,
and R the remaining reserves. There is no eligible pharmacy inventory in the
stated cases. Let h be the shared percentage, k the perishables percentage and
M the scripts dollar cap.

The shared cap's denominator retains that cap and removes the perishables cap.
Its counterfactual base satisfies

    B_without_P = U + P + min(S, M, h * B_without_P) - R

Consequently the scripts contribution is

    min(S, M, h / (1 - h) * (U + P - R)).

The perishables denominator removes the shared cap, retaining its own cap:

    B_without_shared = U + S + min(P, k * B_without_shared) - R.

Its contribution is min(P, k / (1 - k) * (U + S - R)). The final borrowing base
sums U and these two contributions, then subtracts R. Applying both percentages
to the same uncapped total does not implement the source definitions. Reserves
also affect each percentage denominator. The separate pharmacy-inventory cap
has no numerical effect because its eligible balance is explicitly zero.

The loan limit is min(commitment, borrowing base). Existing revolving exposure
includes cash loans and letters of credit. Neither the letter-of-credit nor
swingline subfacility adds to aggregate commitment.

## Purchase decision and sharp endpoint

A purchase of cost x creates regular-inventory advance value u*x and perishable
advance value p*x, where u and p follow from the mix, appraisals and applicable
seasonal rate. The same purchase needs x of new cash borrowing. Advance value
is collateral capacity, not cash proceeds from selling inventory.

The post-purchase borrowing base is the sum of one affine expression, the
minimum of three affine scripts expressions, and the minimum of two affine
perishables expressions. Equivalently it is the minimum of their six paired
affine expressions A_j + b_j*x. Funding requires every inequality

    existing_exposure + x <= A_j + b_j*x

and existing_exposure + x <= commitment. Initial feasibility is checked. For
b_j below one, the inequality gives an upper bound
(A_j - existing_exposure) / (1 - b_j). For b_j at least one it adds no upper
bound on nonnegative x because the initial position is feasible. The minimum
of the finite upper bounds and commitment headroom is the exact maximum.
Every inequality is closed. The endpoint is feasible, and any larger purchase
violates at least one binding inequality, so a tighter or larger frontier is
unsupported.

The Decimal oracle uses this branch envelope. Alternative SQL finds the endpoint
by 60 steps of feasibility bisection and calculates the certificate from the
counterfactual bases. The independent test applies the two contractual fixed
points with exact rational arithmetic, checks the value at each endpoint, and
checks feasible and infeasible purchases immediately on either side. The
feasible set is an interval because the borrowing base is concave in x.

## Controls and limitations

Controls change reserves, letters of credit, purchase mix, lender commitment,
the shared dollar cap and seasonal rate. Monetary scaling and row order preserve
the expected relationships. Unused subfacility changes leave all answers
unchanged. Financial negatives target uncapped borrowing, incorrect percentage
denominators, omitted reserve effects, added subfacility capacity, omitted
letters of credit, frozen collateral and purchase cost confused with advance
value. Constant original answers fail recomputation controls.

Optional prose and allocation witnesses are not semantically graded. The task
uses the existing frozen scorer and unchanged execution resources. Independent
human source and legal review remains pending.
