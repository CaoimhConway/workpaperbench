# Customer collections through a receivables-financing transition

The case measures construction of a cash-flow classification model across receivables sales with retained interests and secured borrowing. Its nearest counterparts are workflow-v15/a07 and b10 for cash generation, c07 for comparability, and workflow-hard-pilot-v1/b01 for a cash-to-asset bridge. The additional decisions are how retained purchase-price interests, customer collections, recycled sale proceeds and purchaser capital interact. A positive change in reported operating cash does not establish the same change on a consistent NER-facility basis.

The accounting change was real. Before the amendment, NER sold receivables for cash plus a subordinated interest called DPP. Receipts on DPP appeared in investing cash. After the amendment, receivables remain recognized, customer cash flows are operating, and facility borrowing cash flows are financing. Receivables of 1,529 were reassigned in exchange for forgiving the remaining DPP on December 31, 2024. That asset exchange is not a cash receipt and is outside the prior nine-month window.

The analytical presentation adjusts only NER. Separate tariff-based receivables sales were 3,207 in 2025 and 228 in 2024, and their timing effects are not established by these gross sale figures. Accordingly, the result is not companywide factoring-neutral cash generation or total customer collections. No forecast, zero credit loss or undisclosed invoice-collection observation is assumed.

## Cash classification model

The old program's disclosed 2024 flows are:

| Flow | USD million |
| --- | ---: |
| Receivables transferred | 9,370 |
| Proceeds from new transfers | 1,688 |
| Customer collections on DPP | 7,404 |
| Collections reinvested in the facility | 9,092 |
| Net cash principal payment to purchasers | 300 |
| Net DPP investing cash | 7,104 |
| Noncash DPP creation | 7,682 |

The 9,092 reinvested total is the sum of transfer proceeds and DPP collections. It is not a further independent cash receipt. The old presentation already recognizes the 1,688 cash transfer proceeds in operating cash. Moving all 9,092 into operating cash again would double-count those proceeds.

An independent treasury view conserves the same funds:

`old program cash = 1,688 + 7,104 = 8,792`

`customer receipts less net purchaser principal = 9,092 - 300 = 8,792`

The required operating reclassification is therefore the 7,404 gross DPP customer receipts. The 300 repayment belongs in financing cash. Moving only the 7,104 net investing line to operating cash would leave the purchaser repayment inside the measure of operating generation.

For 2024, the classification entries are:

| Entry | Operating | Investing | Financing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| DPP customer collections | +7,404 | -7,404 | 0 |
| Purchaser principal payment | 0 | +300 | -300 |
| Total adjustment | +7,404 | -7,104 | -300 |

The 2025 secured-borrowing presentation already classifies these economic types as requested. Its DPP line is zero, and no additional adjustment is made. The construction does not treat the zero year-end facility balances as proof that there were no gross intra-period borrowing transactions. Such transactions would already be financing cash under the amended arrangement.

The signed collateral cash item falls from +1,495 received to -192 posted, a change of -1,687. Excluding this item gives operating cash of 4,461 in 2024 and 3,624 in 2025, a remaining decline of 837. This residual change is not attributed to a particular operational cause.

## Retained-interest model

The reported retained-interest stock is the fair value of the transferred receivable pool less purchaser funding. At December 31, 2023, `1,516 - 300 = 1,216`. At September 30, 2024, `1,766 - 0 = 1,766`.

New retained consideration implied by the transfers is `9,370 - 1,688 = 7,682`, matching the independent noncash DPP disclosure. Customer collections reduce the retained claim. A reduction in purchaser funding increases the company's retained interest even without collecting another customer invoice. The funding stock changes by `0 - 300 = -300`.

The disclosed transactions explain DPP movement of `7,682 - 7,404 - (-300) = 578`. Reported DPP rises by `1,766 - 1,216 = 550`. The unallocated movement is `550 - 578 = -28`.

| Required retained-interest measure | USD million |
| --- | ---: |
| new_dpp_from_transfers | 7,682 |
| funding_change | -300 |
| reported_dpp_change | 550 |
| explained_dpp_change | 578 |
| unallocated_dpp_change | -28 |

There is a materially different independent route. The receivable pool rises by `1,766 - 1,516 = 250`. Transfers less recycled collections imply a pool increase of `9,370 - 9,092 = 278`. Their difference is again -28. Funding shifts allocate that same pool between the company and purchasers, rather than explain away the pool discrepancy.

The 43 loss-on-sale observation is not a complete valuation roll-forward. It cannot be substituted for the net residual of -28. The task does not require assigning that residual to credit losses, valuation changes or another missing component. A valid explanation may discuss possible causes, but optional prose is not semantically graded.

## References, alternatives and controls

The executable Decimal oracle in `scripts/workflow_hard_receivables.py` applies cash classification entries and independently models the DPP stock. Reference SQL constructs recast category totals directly. The accepted alternative pivots a journal of reclassification entries and derives retained-interest changes through the receivable-pool route. The latter can use the transfer/reinvestment facts without citing the redundant noncash DPP observation, so that sufficient evidence path is explicitly accepted.

`tests/test_hard_receivables.py` checks exact rational treasury conservation, receivable-pool allocation, both SQL routes and the original outputs. All eight controls remain internally coherent: proportional monetary scaling, row reordering, additional customer collections, a net purchaser funding draw replacing the repayment, noncash originations, a separate unallocated valuation movement, collateral cash and capex cash. In particular, funding changes affect financing cash and the retained-interest split without changing customer cash generation. Noncash changes do not generate cash.

Eight incorrect submissions fail numerically: treating net DPP investing cash as gross collections, counting recycled transfer proceeds twice, treating noncash DPP creation as cash, reversing principal cash direction, counting reassigned receivables as cash, omitting funding changes from DPP, forcing the residual to zero and substituting the disclosed loss for the residual.

The original claim verdicts are contradicted, supported and unresolved. Consistent operating cash declined, total cash is preserved, and the cash-timing benefit of separate tariff sales is not identified. Claims apply only to the original dossier, while SQL is recomputed on controls.
