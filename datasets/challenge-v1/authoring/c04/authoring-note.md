# Private authoring note

The original Visa FY2024 10-K provides annual revenue for years ended September 30 and nominal payments volume for twelve months ended June 30. The same 10-K explicitly says annual service revenue for September years was based on client-reported June-ended volume for the corresponding year. The FY2024 release distinguishes June volume underlying Q4 service revenue from current September-quarter volume. The source also reports processed transactions for the September fiscal year, which are not payment volume.

Independent Decimal reference values: service revenue growth 8.687440982058545%, lagged June nominal payment-volume growth 7.421196326631919%, difference 1.2662446554266271 percentage points, FY2024 aggregate service-revenue-to-lagged-volume proxy 0.12410659272951324%. The proxy denominator is USD billion and the numerator USD million, with the scale conversion by 1,000. It is not a per-transaction rate or direct contractual-price observation.

Controls preserve disclosed-line arithmetic. The first adds USD 500 million to FY2024 service revenue and net revenue. The second adds USD 100 billion to FY2023 nominal payment volume. These are synthetic changed inputs. The first changes revenue growth, growth gap and proxy. The second changes payment-volume growth and growth gap. Each calculated claim changes under at least one control.

Wrong approaches include dividing September-year revenue by September-ended nominal payment volume, using processed transactions as payment volume, mixing USD million with USD billion, treating published rounded constant-dollar growth as nominal growth, or omitting negative client incentives from net revenue context. A candidate-only shortcut is copying a stated 7% or 8% reported growth rate rather than computing the requested nominal period comparison. The conclusion is deliberately limited: aggregate proxy movement alone does not identify contractual pricing because the underlying scope and timing are aggregate and FX-converted.
