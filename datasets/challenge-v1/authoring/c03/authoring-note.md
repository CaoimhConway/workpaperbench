The original Block FY2024 10-K reports Bitcoin revenue and Bitcoin costs separately for FY2024 and its FY2023 comparative. Bitcoin revenue is a gross sale amount. The analytical Bitcoin gross-profit amount is revenue minus its matched Bitcoin cost, not sales growth. FY2023 is taken from the same original filing. The later 2025 Bitcoin ecosystem metric or any later reclassification is outside this dossier.

Controls are synthetic and preserve table arithmetic. `bitcoin_sales_change` adds USD 50,000 thousand to FY2024 Bitcoin revenue and total net revenue. `bitcoin_cost_change_2024` adds USD 80,000 thousand to FY2024 Bitcoin cost and total cost of revenue. `bitcoin_cost_change_2023` adds USD 25,000 thousand to FY2023 Bitcoin cost and total cost of revenue. Every calculated claim changes in at least one control. All percentage shares and Cash App metrics are left as reported context.

Wrong approaches include reporting Bitcoin revenue as Bitcoin profit, subtracting total cost of revenue instead of Bitcoin costs, mixing cost rows across fiscal years, treating the Cash App percentages as consolidated shares, or treating the derived margin as a separately reported GAAP segment line. A candidate-only shortcut is copying the reported 7% Bitcoin revenue growth to the gross-profit growth claim, which the source costs disprove.
